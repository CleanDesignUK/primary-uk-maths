# Matchday Maths Coach learning plan

## Research behind the changes

Use a short assessment to find out what the pupil knows, then make the feedback specific enough to act on. An incorrect answer can arise from several causes, including a slip, an unfamiliar representation or an insecure method. Ask a smaller, relevant question before changing the pupil’s practice. [EEF maths guidance for KS2 and KS3](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/maths-ks-2-3) · [EEF early maths guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/early-maths) · [EEF feedback toolkit](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/index.php/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/feedback)

Check the skills in the order they support the mathematics. For example, the Year 3 guidance connects column methods to place value and proceeds from simpler calculations to exchanging. Start with one of these component skills when the pupil has trouble with the combined method. [DfE primary maths teaching guidance for England](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/teaching-mathematics-in-primary-schools) · [NCETM Year 3 curriculum map](https://www.ncetm.org.uk/classroom-resources/cp-year-3-curriculum-map/) · [NCETM column subtraction teaching guidance](https://www.ncetm.org.uk/media/vgkk1b4w/ncetm_mm_sp1_y3_se21_teach.pdf)

Pair a worked example with a new problem and revisit the skill over time. Use diagrams and short, systematic instruction where they help pupils connect the meaning to the symbols. [IES elementary maths intervention guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/26) · [IES study and instruction guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1)

Research on maths games reports varied learning outcomes. Tie the football feedback to a clear mathematical action and evaluate success on a later maths task as well as how often children choose to play. [Tokac, Novak and Thompson’s maths games meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1111/JCAL.12347)

## What the game does now

| Situation | Coach’s decision |
|---|---|
| A pupil misses or skips a shot | Show the working. Use that player’s recent independent answers to decide whether to check a foundation or revisit the skill. |
| A relevant foundation is uncertain | Use one of the remaining shots for a short check. Prefer a foundation with recent difficulty over an untested one. In mixed division practice, check the table used in the missed question. |
| The pupil answers the check independently | Leave other shots in between, then offer a fresh problem on the original skill if there is a suitable one and enough shots remain. |
| The check is wrong, skipped or needs help | Defer the harder skill for the rest of the match. Recommend a three-shot untimed drill. Two different independent answers at the end of that drill can lead back to the original skill. |
| The foundations have recent independent evidence | Keep practice at the original level. A fresh second-chance question gives the pupil another opportunity to use the method. |

The first Coach match can follow up a missed warm-up item. Coach uses up to two in-match checks and two fresh replays within the existing shot count. Manual practice, the dedicated tables challenge and assessment question sets keep their own focus. Foundation checks, second-chance shots and untimed drills award no career XP. They still provide feedback and learning evidence.

For example, after a mistake on **864 − 788**, Coach might check **14 − 8** if there is little evidence on subtracting across 10. If the pupil has already shown that skill but struggles with place value, the check can focus on digit value instead. A successful check is a reason to try a different column subtraction. A difficult check sends the pupil to the smaller skill with the full column working available to review.

Coach looks at the last five answers to a foundation from the previous 30 days. Three distinct independent correct answers with at most one difficulty are enough to bypass a check. Two distinct difficulties making up at least half the sample, or a difficult recent Coach check, can trigger a practice recommendation. These thresholds are game design choices to test with pupils. The separate match-fit rules still require evidence across days.

## Teaching order across Years 1 to 6

The teaching map covers all 247 digital skill families. It connects concepts within and across the year groups. The first entry for a skill is the usual starting check. The other entries give Coach relevant alternatives when the pupil has already demonstrated that step or when another step has stronger evidence of difficulty. Starter skills such as counting objects or recognising shapes begin their own sequences. [Department for Education primary mathematics guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/teaching-mathematics-in-primary-schools) · [NCETM curriculum prioritisation](https://ncetm.org.uk/classroom-resources/cp-curriculum-prioritisation-in-primary-maths/)

| Strand | Example route from earlier to later practice |
|---|---|
| Written subtraction | Count objects → subtract within 10 → subtract across 10 → two-digit exchange → larger column calculations |
| Multiplication | Equal groups → 2 times table → 4 times table → 8 times table → mixed multiplication and inverse division |
| Fractions | Equal parts → read fractions → same-denominator calculations → different denominators → mixed numbers |
| Time | Read hours and half hours → five-minute intervals → durations → time conversions and timetable questions |
| Position | Read positions → first-quadrant coordinates → four-quadrant coordinates → reflection and translation |
| Data | Read simple charts → compare data → interpret line graphs or use fractions and angle facts to read pie charts |

The pupil starts at their selected practice year. Repeated difficulty can trigger a check of an earlier step. Coach traces backwards through steps with recorded difficulty, stopping when it reaches a step that still needs checking. The untimed drills keep the route so a pupil can work forwards one skill at a time. The original year remains the focus of the practice plan. A check or a successful short drill provides evidence to guide the next question. Secure status still depends on independent performance across days.

## Prioritised improvements

1. **Make checks more specific to the error.** Start with column exchange, division remainders and fraction denominators. Ask pupils to show the value being exchanged, the next division step or the equivalent fraction they need. Review the prompts and sample responses with primary teachers before using them to choose a route.
2. **Build three-step coaching sequences.** Show one concise worked example with a ten frame, place-value counters or a fraction strip. Let the pupil complete one missing step. Then give a fresh problem to solve alone. Keep “I don’t know” visible throughout and record independent success separately.
3. **Carry second chances into later sessions.** Schedule a new version of the repaired skill the next day and a week later. Include a different representation or short word problem so the pupil has to recognise when the skill applies. Surface that return in the Play lobby.
4. **Deepen the times-table pathway.** Teach a usable connection, such as doubling a known 4-times fact to find an 8-times fact. Check direct multiplication, a missing factor and both inverse divisions before mixed recall. Use a short accuracy round before a timed challenge where recall is still developing.
5. **Extend and audit the prerequisite map.** Work through the remaining Year 1–6 families against the DfE and NCETM sequences. Start with decimal place value, units and time, then geometry and statistics. Keep the grown-up report clear about which evidence came from independent answers, hints and practical observations.
6. **Reward the return to a hard skill.** Try a small football celebration or comeback badge when a pupil independently solves the original type of problem on a later day. Keep rewards for easier checks and hints separate from competitive records. Compare completion, help use, next-day accuracy and one-week transfer across years and reading preferences.
